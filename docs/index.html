Elastic releases, TCO, and Serverless

Why stay on a supported path

  • Security fixes and vendor support follow supported versions—EOL dates are hard deadlines.
  • Elastic Agent, integrations, and Kibana capabilities track the stack; lag creates breakage and missing features.
  • Each release ships measurable gains in storage, query, and ingest—see next slide.

Serverless and reserved capacity

  • Self-managed clusters often run headroom (peak + HA + growth)—you pay for capacity that sits idle much of the time.
  • Serverless aligns cost with usage (consumption-based billing); in TCO models that strip out unused reserve, teams often see ~50% lower effective spend versus “always-on” reserved sizing.

Choose deliberately

  • Stay self-managed when you need full control of topology, plugins, and on-prem compliance—and accept recurring upgrade programs.
  • Move to Serverless when you want Elastic to operate scale and upgrades and you can align networking and procurement with Elastic Cloud.
  • Prove it: pilot one workload; compare TCO (cash + engineering time) and risk side by side.
