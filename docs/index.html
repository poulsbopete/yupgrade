Why stay on a supported path

  • Security fixes and support follow supported versions—EOL dates are firm.
  • Agent, integrations, and Kibana track the stack; lag means breakage and gaps.
  • Each release ships measurable gains in storage, query, and ingest—next slide.

What you get in each release

  • Use blookot/elastic-releases as a compact, version-by-version map (ES, Kibana, Agent, etc.) with links to docs and blogs.
  • Minors (e.g. 8.14 → 8.19): mostly additive fixes and features; upgrade steps follow Elastic’s published upgrade paths (which hops are supported).
  • Majors (e.g. 9.0): bigger platform shifts—e.g. Lucene 10 and cleanup of deprecated surfaces—plan with the Upgrade Assistant and prepare-to-upgrade guidance.

From 8.x to 9—or to Serverless

  • Self-managed → 9.x: move Elasticsearch + Kibana along the required 8.last milestone first (Elastic’s examples use the latest 8.19 before jumping to 9.x), then your target 9.x—snapshot, Upgrade Assistant, then upgrade.
  • Serverless: provision an Elastic Cloud Serverless project and migrate (snapshot/restore, reindex, or dual-write)—it’s a platform move, not an in-place cluster version change.

Serverless and reserved capacity

  • Self-managed clusters hold headroom for peak, HA, and growth—you pay for idle capacity.
  • Serverless ties cost to usage; internal TCO models often land near ~50% lower effective spend vs. always-on reserved sizing.

Choose deliberately

  • Self-managed for full control of topology, plugins, and on-prem compliance—plus recurring upgrade programs.
  • Serverless when Elastic should run scale and upgrades and Cloud fits your network and procurement.
  • Pilot one workload; compare TCO and risk with real data.
